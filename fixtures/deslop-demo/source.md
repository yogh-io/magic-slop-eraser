# On the word "slop"

I read a post this morning by a man who said he came to eat his words out loud in public. He said one year ago, he wrote a post saying, "AI will not kill writing because companies will always pay for good quality writing." It's got a ton of views.

He assumed companies will always want good writing. Except he just lost his job to AI. So he's quitting writing and going back to programming. He admitted he was wrong. Companies don't want good writing. They're fine with slop.

This isn't about AI. It's about calling it slop and it's about human writing.

Slop was the dictionary's word of the year last year. Every time they pick a word of the year, they explain why they picked it. So they said, "Amid all the talk about AI threats, slop sets a tone that's less fearful and more mocking."

That's how we use the word to mock AI. But it's a little deeper. It's about mocking the people using it too, isn't it? Like we're the real writers and they're just using AI churning out slop.

So we can roll our eyes at them and get on with our human writing.

They went on to say that in the 1700s slop used to mean soft mud. Then it meant food waste like pig slop, and today it's a generalization for rubbish, for something that's not very good or has little to no value. And then - the final blow. They said when we use the word slop, it's like sending a little message to AI, but when it comes to replacing human creativity, it's not that intelligent. Not good.

The problem with calling AI slop is that it creates a subconscious divide that says AI is bad and therefore human is good. But the truth is a lot of human writing isn't very good. And I'm sorry to say that, but it's true. Every human writer has pieces they knocked out of the park and pieces that suck. The ratio is the only issue. A skilled writer with years of experience might write eight good pieces for every two that suck. A writer who hasn't worked on their skill set is likely the other way round.
